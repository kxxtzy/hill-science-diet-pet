## What Hill's Science Diet is, and how it differs from Prescription Diet

Hill's Pet Nutrition is an American pet food company owned by Colgate-Palmolive. The brand's own history traces back to 1939, when a veterinarian started formulating food for a dog with kidney problems — which is a neat origin story, but the practical thing to know is that Hill's splits its range into two families that are not interchangeable.

**Science Diet** is everyday nutrition, sold openly in pet shops, supermarkets and online. **Prescription Diet** is therapeutic and sits behind veterinary authorisation — things like k/d, i/d, c/d and z/d, which target kidney, digestive, urinary and allergy conditions. If your vet wrote one of those letters on a slip, you want Prescription Diet. If they said "feed him a good quality food", you're in Science Diet territory.

Hill's markets Science Diet as the brand vets feed their own pets, and the "veterinarian recommended" line shows up in retailer copy everywhere. It's a marketing claim rather than a neutral third-party ranking, worth knowing when you're weighing it against Royal Canin or a newer premium brand. In Singapore, Hill's Science Diet has been among the leading dog food brands by value, alongside Royal Canin and Wellness, so the recommendation isn't coming from nowhere.

## Choosing a formula in about two minutes

Three questions, in this order. Life stage first, then size, then special need. Getting these in the wrong order is how people end up buying puppy food for a seven-year-old dog or paying a premium for small-breed kibble their labrador doesn't need.

**Life stage.** Puppy and Kitten formulas for growth. Adult for roughly 1–6 years for dogs. Then 7+ versions for mature dogs, and for cats there's a senior tier that goes further, including an 11+ line. Cats hit "senior" earlier than most owners expect — the switch is usually around 7, not 10.

**Size.** Small and mini breeds get their own kibble size and calorie density. The small-breed range covers dogs up to around 25 pounds (11 kg) at adult weight. Large breed formulas exist for the opposite problem. This matters more than it sounds: a Chihuahua trying to eat large-breed kibble will leave half the bowl, and a labrador on small-breed food is getting a kibble too small to slow it down.

**Special need.** Sensitive Stomach & Skin for dogs that react badly to changes. Perfect Weight and Light for dogs that need fewer calories — the Perfect Weight small and toy breed formula is marketed as clinically proven for weight management. Hairball Control and Indoor for cats. Lamb & Rice as an alternative protein for dogs that do better off chicken.

## What it actually costs to feed, over a year

Weigh the bag against how often you buy it rather than against a single receipt. A 7 kg bag for a five-kilo dog lasts a very different length of time than the same bag for a thirty-kilo dog, which is why per-kilogram pricing is the only fair comparison when you're looking at two different pack sizes on the same page.

For small breeds, the 1.5–2 kg packs look cheap until you work out the per-kg rate, which is often double what the 7 kg bag costs per kilogram. If storage is the only thing stopping you from buying bigger, remember that dry food has a shelf life and goes stale once opened — splitting a large bag into sealed containers is the usual workaround.

For cats, the calculation is less dramatic because the packs are smaller and consumption is steadier, which is also why "buy the big bag" advice doesn't transfer cleanly from dogs to cats.

## A five-minute routine before every pet food order

1. **Decide the formula and size first.** Cashback doesn't rescue a wrong purchase, and returns on food are often restricted for safety reasons.
2. **Compare the sticker price across two or three sellers.** Check the per-kilogram rate, not the headline price.
3. **Then check the cashback rate for each of those sellers, on the day.** Rates fluctuate and boosted offers are time-limited.

## Where cashback quietly disappears

Most failed cashback claims aren't disputes — they're small procedural mistakes. The ones worth knowing:

- **Third-party coupon sites mid-checkout.** Applying a code from another site can overwrite the tracking.
- **Gift cards.** Buying them, and often paying with them, is excluded from earning.
- **Ad blockers and privacy extensions.** They can strip the tracking parameters before you reach the merchant.
- **Switching devices or browsers mid-session.** Start and finish on the same one.
- **Expecting confirmed status immediately.** Purchases usually sit in a pending state until the merchant validates the order, which is why the payout isn't instant.
- **Assuming every seller is on the platform.** If your preferred pet shop isn't listed, there's no rate to earn, and no amount of refreshing will change that.
